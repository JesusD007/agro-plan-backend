## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid — structured proposal generation and content generation.

**Description:**
The .NET Worker uses Gemini to help a farm owner or administrator review a sowing-plan proposal and planting-frame options. Good output is clearly marked as a draft, grounded in validated farm/crop inputs and source-attributed climate data, identifies missing information, and never confirms a farm record or invents deterministic agronomic values.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. The model must not invent crop parameters, climate facts, planting dates, or numeric recommendations when validated inputs are missing.
2. AI output must remain a reviewable draft; no farm plan or planting frame becomes confirmed without explicit owner/administrator approval.
3. The worker must not leak the Gemini API key, private farm data, credentials, or full logbook content through clients, logs, or unrelated farm jobs.
4. Invalid, stale, unsupported, or low-confidence provider output must be rejected or returned with missing-data/error state, never silently persisted as authoritative data.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Smallholder agriculture and crop planning in the Dominican Republic.

**User Population:** Farm owners and farm administrators who plan crop cycles, planting layout, and field activities; agronomists or agricultural extension specialists validate crop data and review high-impact recommendations.

**Stakes Level:** High — a misleading planting window, water assumption, or planting layout can waste seed, labor, water, or a growing season and reduce farm income.

**Output Consequence:** A user may schedule a crop cycle or choose a planting layout based on the draft. The pilot therefore requires explicit user confirmation, source-aware climate context, deterministic calculations from validated inputs, and abstention when essential data is missing.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

<!-- Domain-specific rubric ingredients — in practitioner language, not AI jargon -->
<!-- Format: Dimension / Good (expert accepts) / Bad (expert flags) / Stakes / Source -->

**Dimension: Local crop and planting-window fit**  
Good: A proposal names the crop and variety, the farm's locality, the intended planting date, the source and validity of its calendar evidence, and explains any regional or seasonal restriction.  
Bad: It treats a national or older calendar as universally valid, mixes regions or varieties, or recommends a date without identifying the evidence used.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Dominican Ministry of Agriculture, 2025 contingency plan and crop calendar; FAO crop-water guidance notes that growing period depends on variety, climate, and planting date and recommends local data.

**Dimension: Water and weather feasibility**  
Good: The draft reflects the farm's stated water source and availability, shows the date and source of climate information, distinguishes observed conditions from forecast, and flags drought or severe-weather uncertainty.  
Bad: It assumes irrigation or rainfall that the farmer did not report, presents stale weather as current, or proceeds as if a climate source were available when it failed.  
Stakes: High  
Source: The local interview describes both well-based irrigation and rain-dependent planting, including drought-related pauses; Ministry of Agriculture contingency planning covers drought and hydrometeorological risks.

**Dimension: Planting-frame capacity and field access**  
Good: Capacity is calculated from usable parcel area, access lanes, and a validated crop/variety spacing rule; the result explains the inputs and the farmer can compare and confirm options.  
Bad: It applies one spacing value to every crop or parcel, counts unusable area as plantable, or emits a precise count when dimensions or validated spacing are missing.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Phase requirement REQ-rf-g-11; the local interview gives one plantain spacing example but explicitly says practice varies.

**Dimension: Evidence and missing agronomic inputs**  
Good: Each factual crop parameter has a source, region/variety applicability, validation status, and validity period; missing values are named and unsupported numeric recommendations are withheld.  
Bad: The model fills gaps with plausible values, silently converts estimates into catalog facts, or obscures which facts came from the user versus a source.  
Stakes: High  
Source: User-confirmed Phase 1 decisions D-07–D-09; FAO crop-water guidance prioritizes local data over indicative defaults.

**Dimension: Reviewability before action**  
Good: The proposal states its important factors, missing data, uncertainty, source references, and draft status; only the owner or authorized farm administrator can confirm it.  
Bad: It presents a recommendation as an approved plan or leaves the user unable to tell why it was suggested.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Pilot permission matrix and requirements REQ-pilot-sowing-plan / REQ-pilot-planting-frame.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Regional-calendar mismatch:** a season/date associated with one Dominican production zone or variety is applied to a different farm, even though the Ministry's crop calendar identifies production zones and local restrictions may change by region and year.
2. **Water-source mismatch:** the proposal assumes reliable irrigation or rainfall despite a rain-fed parcel, limited well access, or a drought-related planting pause.
3. **False precision in planting density:** a spacing example or unvalidated variety rule is turned into an exact plant count without checking parcel boundaries, lanes, terrain, or usable area.
4. **Stale or missing climate treated as current:** an unavailable or old weather observation/forecast is presented as evidence for a current planting decision.
5. **Unverified agronomy presented as fact:** model-generated crop duration, spacing, water need, or planting window is mistaken for locally validated agronomist guidance.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No agriculture-specific rule governing AI-generated planting advice was identified in the sources reviewed. The service processes user-account and identifiable producer information, so Dominican Law No. 172-13 is relevant to those personal-data flows; obtain legal review before production, including Gemini-provider handling. The pilot does not prescribe pesticide or fertilizer use.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct Google GenAI SDK (`Google.GenAI` for C#), behind an AgroPlan provider adapter; no higher-level agent framework.

**Version:** Unpinned. Verify the official `Google.GenAI` release, NuGet metadata, and .NET target compatibility immediately before installation; do not guess a package version.

**Rationale:**
Phase 1 requires bounded Gemini calls that produce reviewable proposals inside the .NET Worker, not autonomous agents, RAG, or multi-agent orchestration. Deterministic agronomic calculations, approval, and persisted job state stay in AgroPlan; the model cannot confirm farm data.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Microsoft Agent Framework | Adds orchestration overhead for the pilot's bounded, non-agentic proposals; reconsider only if later phases require stateful tool orchestration. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — Gemini is user-selected for the pilot; isolate model calls behind an adapter and keep the model configurable.

## 3. Framework Quick Reference

> Fetched from official docs by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Distilled for this specific use case.

### Installation
```bash

```

### Core Imports
```csharp
using System.Text.Json;
using System.Text.Json.Nodes;
using Google.GenAI;
using Google.GenAI.Types;
```

### Entry Point Pattern
```csharp
// Register this adapter in the Worker DI container. Supply GEMINI_API_KEY and
// GEMINI_MODEL through server-side configuration/secrets, never through a client.
public sealed class GeminiProposalClient(IConfiguration configuration)
{
    public async Task<string> GenerateDraftJsonAsync(
        string validatedFarmInputsJson,
        CancellationToken cancellationToken)
    {
        var apiKey = configuration["GEMINI_API_KEY"]
            ?? throw new InvalidOperationException("Gemini API key is not configured.");
        var model = configuration["GEMINI_MODEL"]
            ?? throw new InvalidOperationException("Gemini model is not configured.");

        var client = new Client(apiKey: apiKey);
        var config = new GenerateContentConfig
        {
            SystemInstruction = new Content
            {
                Parts = [new Part { Text =
                    "Create a reviewable proposal from the supplied validated facts. " +
                    "Do not invent agronomic or weather values. Mark unavailable facts as missing." }]
            },
            Temperature = 0.1f,
            MaxOutputTokens = 1200,
            ResponseMimeType = "application/json",
            // Keep schema in config, not duplicated in the prompt.
            ResponseJsonSchema = JsonNode.Parse("""
                {"type":"object","properties":{
                  "summary":{"type":"string"},
                  "missingInputs":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"}},
                  "sourceIds":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"}}
                },"required":["summary","missingInputs","sourceIds"]}
                """)
        };

        var response = await client.Models.GenerateContentAsync(
            model: model,
            contents: validatedFarmInputsJson,
            config: config,
            cancellationToken: cancellationToken);

        var json = response.Candidates?.FirstOrDefault()?.Content?.Parts?
            .FirstOrDefault()?.Text;
        if (string.IsNullOrWhiteSpace(json))
            throw new InvalidOperationException("Gemini returned no proposal content.");

        // Parse into an application DTO and run domain validation before persistence.
        using var parsed = JsonDocument.Parse(json);
        return parsed.RootElement.GetRawText();
    }
}
```

This is a provider-adapter pattern, not an autonomous agent. The Worker must still deserialize to its proposal DTO, validate required fields and provenance, and persist only a draft. The example schema intentionally contains no agronomic recommendations.

### Key Abstractions
<!-- Framework-specific concepts the developer must understand before coding -->
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `Client` | SDK client for the Gemini Developer API; initialized with the server key. | Create/configure inside the Worker adapter and keep credentials out of API responses and logs. |
| `Models.GenerateContentAsync` | Asynchronous single-turn content generation returning `GenerateContentResponse`. | Use for bounded draft generation; pass the operation's `CancellationToken`. |
| `GenerateContentConfig` | Per-request controls such as system instruction, temperature, output limit, MIME type, and response schema. | Keep generation constraints explicit and centrally configured. |
| `ResponseJsonSchema` | JSON Schema constraint for JSON-mode responses, supplied as `JsonNode`. | Request a stable proposal shape; it does not prove that values are factually valid. |
| `GenerateContentResponse` | Candidate content plus response metadata, including possible usage and finish information. | Reject empty/blocked/incomplete output; retain safe telemetry for cost and diagnosis. |

### Common Pitfalls
<!-- Gotchas specific to this framework and system type — from docs, issues, and community reports -->
1. **JSON Schema is not semantic validation.** Gemini supports a subset of JSON Schema, and valid-shaped output can still contain unsupported claims; apply application and deterministic domain validation before saving any draft.
2. **Keep schema ordering explicit when using `ResponseSchema`.** Its dictionary-backed properties do not guarantee order. Prefer `ResponseJsonSchema` with `JsonNode` (or set `PropertyOrdering`) for predictable schemas; do not duplicate the schema in prompt text.
3. **Cancellation must reach the SDK call.** Pass the Worker's `CancellationToken` to `GenerateContentAsync`; an abandoned job that continues generating can consume quota and incur cost.
4. **Retry only transient failures.** Rate limits and transient 5xx responses need bounded exponential backoff; invalid credentials, malformed requests, blocked content, and quota exhaustion need visible terminal/configuration handling, not an immediate retry loop.
5. **SDK continuation can make multiple requests.** Keep output limits explicit, observe call/usage metadata, and account for every continuation/retry in the job's cost and latency budget.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
Use the official Gemini Developer API through `Google.GenAI`. Keep the model ID in deployment configuration (`GEMINI_MODEL`) so it can be changed without code edits; select and verify a currently available stable model during implementation. Keep `GEMINI_API_KEY` in the Worker deployment's secret store/environment only. Set an explicit low temperature for repeatable drafts and a bounded `MaxOutputTokens`; neither setting establishes factual correctness. Record the configured model and non-sensitive response usage metadata with each job.

**Core Pattern:**
The API authorizes the farm and creates an idempotent proposal job. The Worker loads only that farm's validated inputs and source-attributed climate result, then calls a narrow Gemini adapter using a fixed system instruction, structured user data, and a JSON response schema. Deserialize to an application DTO and run domain validation: unsupported parameters remain missing; deterministic values come only from validated rules. Persist the result as a draft with model/config and source references. Require explicit owner/administrator approval before any confirmation transition.

**Tool Use:**
No Gemini function calling or model-controlled tools are needed for this pilot. The model cannot call the database, climate provider, messaging system, or farm APIs. The Worker performs those operations in application code with farm/job authorization and idempotency. Keep deterministic agronomic calculations outside the model.

**State Management:**
PostgreSQL is the source of truth for job state, validated input snapshot/references, proposal draft, approval, and failure state. Queue messages carry stable job identifiers, not binary files or unnecessary farm data. Use the outbox and idempotent Worker processing; retries must not create duplicate proposals. SignalR is a status notification only—the client refetches persisted state from the authorized API.

**Context Window Strategy:**
Send only the authorized, minimal input snapshot required for one proposal: selected crop/cycle, validated parcel facts, applicable validated rule references, and climate facts with source and timestamp if available. Do not include credentials, unrelated farms, or full logbook history. Limit input and output sizes, avoid carrying chat history across jobs, and return missing-input markers instead of filling gaps. Climate provider selection remains a separate planning decision.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with C# DTOs and `System.Text.Json`

Define the response contract as a C# DTO and ask Gemini for JSON constrained by `GenerateContentConfig.ResponseJsonSchema`. Deserialize the candidate with `System.Text.Json`, then validate fields and business meaning in application code. For example, this draft contract carries explanations and missing inputs, never generated numeric crop parameters:

```csharp
public sealed record ProposalDraft(
    string Summary,
    IReadOnlyList<string> MissingInputs,
    IReadOnlyList<string> SourceIds);

public static ProposalDraft ParseAndValidate(string json)
{
    var draft = JsonSerializer.Deserialize<ProposalDraft>(json)
        ?? throw new InvalidDataException("Proposal JSON did not match the contract.");

    if (string.IsNullOrWhiteSpace(draft.Summary) ||
        draft.MissingInputs is null || draft.SourceIds is null)
        throw new InvalidDataException("Proposal is missing required fields.");

    // Further validate source IDs against the job's authorized input snapshot.
    return draft;
}
```

Use `ResponseMimeType = "application/json"` and `ResponseJsonSchema` (the SDK documents `JsonNode` for this schema form). Treat JSON Schema as a shape constraint, not a truth or agronomic-validation layer. If parsing or contract validation fails, make at most one bounded repair attempt with the original authorized inputs and a concise schema-error description; do not ask the model to invent missing data. Log job ID, model, attempt, finish/error category, latency, and safe validation reason—never API keys or raw sensitive farm prompts. If the retry fails, persist a visible failed/review-required state.

### Async-First Design

`GenerateContentAsync` returns a `Task<GenerateContentResponse>` and accepts a `CancellationToken`. Await it from the Worker job handler and pass the host/job token so shutdown or job cancellation stops the SDK operation. Avoid `.Result`, `.Wait()`, and `GetAwaiter().GetResult()`; they block worker threads and can deadlock in synchronization-context based callers. Use ordinary await for schema-constrained proposal output so the full response can be parsed and validated; streaming is for progressive text UX and does not remove the need for final validation.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

Keep a stable system instruction for role, limits, missing-data behavior, and draft status. Put the per-job facts in a separate user message as serialized, validated data. Treat free-text farm input as untrusted data, not instructions; never let it override the system rules. No dynamic retrieval or few-shot examples are required for the pilot; if examples are later added, keep a small reviewed set and version it. Set `MaxOutputTokens` explicitly and keep the response focused on the declared schema.

### Context Window Management

This is a single-turn proposal task, so do not accumulate conversation history. Include only the current authorized job's relevant facts and source IDs. Reject oversized inputs or reduce them deterministically before calling Gemini; do not silently truncate required data. Keep climate source/timestamp attached to weather facts and leave absent climate data missing. The approved crop list does not establish validated agronomic parameters; any unavailable rule stays absent.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Do not publish a numeric per-call estimate until the model, input/output token distribution, and applicable Gemini API price tier are selected. Estimate using observed token usage multiplied by the current official model rates, then set a per-job and monthly budget. Bound output tokens, generation duration, concurrency, and retry count; Google quotas vary by model and project. Track request count, token usage, latency, retry rate, and failure category without logging sensitive prompt content. Exact-match caching is inappropriate for farm-specific proposals unless the cache key includes an authorization-safe, versioned hash of all inputs and rule/source versions; semantic caching is not recommended for decisions that depend on farm facts. Use a cheaper model for any future non-decision-critical routing or summarization only after evaluation shows equivalent quality; this pilot does not require a separate model.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail or 1-5) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| Agronomic grounding and numeric safety | **PASS:** every crop parameter and numeric recommendation is backed by a validated, applicable catalog rule; missing spacing, water, calendar, or variety data is returned as missing. **FAIL:** Gemini supplies an unsupported value or a deterministic calculation uses an unvalidated one. | Code checks for source/validation metadata and rule provenance; agronomist review of examples. | Critical |
| Climate freshness and provenance | **PASS:** every weather fact includes provider, observed/forecast kind, location, and timestamp; stale or unavailable data is surfaced and cannot drive a current recommendation. **FAIL:** old/missing climate is described as current or used without warning. | Code checks freshness/source fields; human review of regional/date fit. | Critical |
| Structured output and task completion | **PASS:** output matches the proposal schema, identifies crop/parcel/cycle, states uncertainty and missing inputs, and remains a draft. **FAIL:** malformed output, mismatched farm/crop, or an incomplete proposal is presented as ready. | Code-based schema and state-transition validation. | High |
| Human approval boundary | **PASS:** generated output is stored as a draft and creates confirmed farm data only after an authorized owner/admin confirmation. **FAIL:** a worker, retry, or model response confirms a plan/frame automatically. | Code-based authorization/state-transition checks; human workflow review. | Critical |
| Explanation and source faithfulness | **PASS:** an agronomist can trace each factual claim to user input, validated catalog data, or climate source and can understand why a value is absent. **FAIL:** the explanation adds unsupported facts, hides uncertainty, or treats an estimate as an approved fact. | Human review by local agronomist/extension specialist and farm owner; no LLM judge until calibrated. | High |
| Privacy, isolation, and prompt-injection resistance | **PASS:** the request only contains the current farm's authorized minimum data; adversarial input cannot reveal another farm, secrets, or internal instructions. **FAIL:** cross-farm data or a credential appears in output, traces, or logs. | Code-based tenant-boundary/redaction checks plus human adversarial review. | Critical |

**Primary Tool:** Promptfoo CLI for repeatable request/response evals; OpenTelemetry with Arize Phoenix for trace inspection. Start with code-based checks and human agronomist review. Do not use an LLM judge until it is calibrated against expert labels.

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** 15 expert-labeled cases initially; expand toward 20 before production.

**Composition:**
Cover the five catalog crops; a supported-input draft; missing spacing/variety/water data; stale and unavailable climate data; rain-fed versus irrigated farms; regional/date mismatch; unusable parcel area/access lanes; invalid or incomplete Gemini JSON; prompt injection; cross-farm identifiers; provider timeout; and a proposal awaiting approval. Use synthetic farm/user records and source-backed agronomic facts only.

**Labeling:**
An agronomist or Ministry extension specialist familiar with the target province labels agronomic fit, acceptable abstention, and factual support. A local producer reviews water feasibility and explanation clarity. Product/engineering labels schema, provenance, privacy, and approval behavior. Record source, region/variety applicability, and catalog version with every expected answer. Resolve disagreements before promoting a case to the reference set; initially use no LLM judge.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Validated-input gate | Required crop/variety/spacing/water inputs lack an approved source or applicable validity dates. | Block numeric recommendation; return the exact missing fields and keep the job in a needs-data state. |
| Climate provenance/freshness | Provider is unavailable, location does not match the farm, timestamp exceeds configured freshness, or observed and forecast data are confused. | Block climate-dependent recommendation; show provider status, timestamp, and retry/continue-without-climate option if permitted. |
| Output schema and claim validation | Gemini response fails schema validation, cites unknown source IDs, contains unsupported numeric claims, or conflicts with deterministic results. | Reject the response; allow at most one bounded repair attempt, then mark the job failed/review-required without persisting a farm record. |
| Authorization and approval | Farm membership is inactive, resource belongs to another farm, or no explicit authorized confirmation exists. | Deny access/confirmation; never persist generated content as confirmed. |
| Secret/data redaction | Prompt or output contains a credential, another farm's data, or prohibited private logbook content. | Drop the response, redact telemetry, and raise a security event for investigation. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Agronomist disagreement / unsupported-claim rate | Review all flagged outputs and a small random sample of confirmed proposals each month; oversample new crops and new catalog versions. | Pause affected crop/model path, correct source catalog or prompt, then relabel affected cases before reactivation. |
| Missing-input and abstention usefulness | Group by crop, region, and missing field; review farmer corrections and repeated retries monthly. | Improve catalog collection or clarify required inputs; do not lower the abstention rule to raise completion. |
| Climate mismatch/staleness | Review provider errors and user reports by location and forecast timestamp. | Reassess freshness policy/provider coverage and keep climate-dependent proposals blocked when evidence is stale. |
| Human override/correction rate | Sample proposals changed or rejected by owners/admins and agronomists. | Inspect the associated source version and evaluation cases; update only with validated evidence. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** OpenTelemetry spans from the .NET Worker/API exported to Arize Phoenix for development and controlled production inspection; keep logs in the existing structured .NET/Render path. Redact prompts and farm content by default.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- Invalid-schema or unsupported-source rejection rate, by model/config version.
- Missing-input/abstention rate, by crop and missing field.
- Climate-provider success rate and age of climate data used by accepted drafts.
- Proposal latency (p50/p95), provider timeout/retry rate, and cost per completed job.
- Unauthorized farm access attempts and any confirmed-record transition without an authorized human action (target: zero).

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Any cross-farm disclosure, secret exposure, or AI-generated confirmed record: stop the affected path and treat as an immediate security/quality incident.
- Any unsupported numeric recommendation detected by the validator: block that proposal; repeated occurrences for a crop/model version disable that path pending agronomist review.
- Alert on sustained provider failure, stale climate data, schema rejection, or p95 latency above the configured service objective; set numeric thresholds after a non-production baseline because no latency or cost SLO is specified yet.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- Always sample abstentions, missing-data cases, provider retries/timeouts, schema repairs, climate near the freshness limit, user edits/rejections, and new crop/model/catalog versions.
- Add a small random sample of routine proposals so monitoring can detect failures that do not trigger an existing signal.
- Store only redacted input features, source IDs/version, output state, and evaluation labels; never retain API keys, full private logbook text, or unnecessary personal data in traces.
